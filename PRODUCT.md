# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Teamfight Tactics players who want fast local board exploration while they are
planning around late-game constraints, often during or near live games. They are
trying to answer concrete "what can I build with this?" questions without
opening a broader, slower browser-based TFT toolbox.

## Product Purpose

This desktop app explores high-cap TFT board compositions from Community Dragon
data. It exists to make constraint-based late-game search fast: users can search
around augments, units, traits, roles, emblems, data source, board size, and
ranking choices, then inspect viable expensive boards quickly.

Success means the interface feels immediate, focused, and trustworthy under
time pressure. It should make board-search constraints visible, keep results
scannable, and avoid pretending to be a comp tier list or full trait tracker.

## Brand Personality

Focused, tactical, and utilitarian. The product should feel like a sharp local
tool for expert exploration: calm enough for repeated use, dense enough to
respect experienced players, and specific enough that every control earns its
place.

## Anti-references

Do not drift into a marketing landing page, a generic SaaS dashboard, a comp
tier-list clone, or a broad TFT companion app. Avoid decorative card grids,
hero-metric layouts, gratuitous animation, and visual noise that slows scanning
during live-game decision making.

## Design Principles

- Keep the search task primary: controls, query state, and results should remain
  legible at a glance.
- Preserve density with order: expert users can handle compact information, but
  alignment, grouping, and state clarity need to do the heavy lifting.
- Optimize for trust under time pressure: loading, errors, data-source state,
  and selection state should be explicit and unambiguous.
- Use game flavor sparingly: Community Dragon assets and TFT terminology carry
  the theme; the UI chrome should stay quiet and operational.
- Prefer local responsiveness over visual novelty: interactions should feel
  fast, predictable, and easy to repeat.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target readable contrast, visible focus states, keyboard-accessible controls,
and hit targets appropriate for a dense desktop app. Respect reduced-motion
preferences, avoid communicating state by color alone, and keep icon/image
labels meaningful for assistive technology.
